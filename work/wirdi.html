CASE STUDY · PERSONAL PRODUCT · FLUTTER

Your daily wird, the Quran and prayer times in one app.

Wirdi (وِردي) brings awrad and adhkar, the Quran with recitations, tafsir and a memorization plan, and prayer times with the adhan into one Arabic and English app. I designed and built it on my own, from the architecture to the native Android parts.

The problem

A daily wird usually means several apps: one for adhkar, one for the Quran, one for prayer times and the adhan. Wirdi puts them in one place, arranged by need: provision, healing, relief from distress, istikhara and more, with a counter for each zikr.

What I built

  • Awrad and your own wird: awrad by need with a counter, favourites and search; a catalogue of 275 adhkar, duas and salawat, each with its hadith source and grade; ready-made morning and evening, after-prayer, sleep and waking adhkar; and a personal wird built from all of it and the Quran, with a reminder at the time you choose.
  • Set up your wird: a short guided flow that asks what you want to pray for (16 needs, from relief and provision to parents and children) and sets up your adhkar, a khatma and a memorization plan in one go.
  • The Quran: a Mushaf page reader, surahs, juz and bookmarks, tafsir ayah by ayah, a khatma planner that adapts the daily portion, a memorization plan with near and far review, sharing an ayah as an image, recitations that keep playing in the background, and live Quran radio from Cairo and Saudi Arabia.
  • Recite aloud: say the page from memory and the app follows you word by word. A Quran-trained speech model runs on the phone, and a matcher I wrote in pure Dart lines up what it hears with the words of the mushaf.
  • Prayer: times for your location, the next prayer with a countdown, a choice of adhan voice, a prayer log with streaks, a Friday card, and a qibla compass.
  • Ramadan: the imsakiyya, imsak and iftar countdowns, a fasting record with the days you owe, tarawih by night, a suhoor reminder, and the adhkar of fasting.
  • Library: a hadith and a sunnah every day, the six hadith books and the forties, 25 stories of the prophets told from the Quran, and a Hijri calendar with occasions.
  • Tasbih and progress: a free counter drawn as a real sebha, bead by bead, in five materials; and a progress page with your run of active days. Everything stays on the phone.
  • One search across the whole app, a guided onboarding and in-app tour, Arabic and English with RTL, light and dark themes, and an in-app notice when a new version is out.

Native Android

Notifications alone could not play a full adhan reliably, so I wrote a local Android plugin in Kotlin. It schedules each prayer with AlarmManager.setAlarmClock, falls back when exact alarms aren’t allowed, and plays the adhan from a foreground media service with a Stop action, even when the app is closed. Workmanager keeps the schedule current from a background isolate.

Seven home-screen widgets are also native Kotlin: next prayer, today’s prayers, the Hijri date, hadith of the day, sunnah of the day, ayah of the day, and an ayah or hadith reminder that can sit on the lock screen. An optional card shows a short reminder each time you unlock the phone, and the volume keys count on the zikr counter.

The Mushaf

The first reader loaded one font per page, which cost memory on every page turn. I rebuilt it to draw all 604 pages from a single Hafs font and per-line word data, with the tajweed-coloured pages kept as an option. Two guard tests protect it: one checks all 6,236 ayat word for word against the source text, the other lays out every page and fails on any overflow.

Content and accessibility

Religious text can’t be approximate. Each dhikr in the catalogue carries its source and grade, and the build script fails if an entry has no source or if a stated virtue isn’t found in its own evidence. Nothing ships until it has been reviewed and approved.

Screen readers can count on the counters and the sebha, hear each Mushaf page as its ayat, and find every button named. Touch targets are at least 48 points, and an accessibility check runs inside the screen tests.

Try it

Download for Android ↓

Wirdi 1.3 is a single APK (127 MB) for Android 7.0 or later on 64-bit ARM phones. It isn’t on Google Play yet, so Android will ask you to allow your browser or file manager to install unknown apps the first time. Allow notifications and alarms when asked so the adhan and wird reminders work. Install steps and the checksum are on the release page ↗.
